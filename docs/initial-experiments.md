# Initial experiments

This document records the protocol for the first experimental suite addressing
the comparison described in [TASK.md](../TASK.md).
The runnable configurations are generated by the
[suite launcher](../scripts/run_suite.py), with shared defaults in the
[training worker](../src/kartstudy/train.py).

## Task and learning protocol

**Environment.** Lighthouse, 1 lap, 1 kart, 0.1-second simulator ticks, maximum
1,500 actions. Simulation race time excludes the startup countdown; official
`finish_time` is used for successful lap times. Timeouts remain failures.

**Observations.** 65 numerical features: velocity, track-center vector and
lateral error, steering limit, distance, and eight upcoming path starts, ends,
and widths. Fixed physical scaling, clipping to [-10,10], zero padding. No
normalization statistics are learned from test data.

**Actions.** Normalized steering, throttle, and brake command in [-1,1]. Throttle
maps to [0,1]; brake is true above zero. Every method has identical controls.
The brake's continuous latent command maps to a binary physical control; this
is a practical approximation for the continuous SAC backbone. Automatic native
game rescue behavior remains shared across methods.

**Reward.** `signed_distance_delta / 10 - 0.1 + 10 * finished`. No opponent rank
term. Signed distance prevents backward/forward cycling from generating free
progress. Return is a learning diagnostic, not a substitute for lap time.
Discount factor 0.99 prioritizes near-term progress; this reward does not imply
exact equivalence to undiscounted minimum-time control.

**Expert.** Team 4 pure-pursuit driving core, lookahead node 2, wheelbase 2.5,
gain 7, curvature-based throttle. Provenance and source commit are in
[expert implementation](../src/kartstudy/expert.py). It is a stateless solo adaptation of the linked
student policy, not a claimed optimal racing controller. Conservative variant
caps throttle at 0.55 after launch. Expert qualification tests Lighthouse,
Zen Garden and Snow Tux Peak; the fast variant fails Zen Garden, so that track
is excluded from training and transfer evaluation. This qualification informed
task selection and must be disclosed.

**Data.** 22 complete laps per expert variant, collection seeds 1000–1021.
Episodes 0–19 are training; 20–21 are validation, excluded from both imitation
and RL replay. Small-data condition takes episodes 0–1. Solo simulation is
deterministic, so independent seeded Gaussian control noise (steer std 0.035,
normalized throttle std 0.02) broadens state coverage. RL transitions retain
executed noisy actions; imitation targets retain the clean expert query at the
same state. All collected laps must finish before the collector accepts the
file. SHA-256 and episode metadata are saved beside each dataset.

**Learners.** Shared actor: 2 hidden layers, 128 ReLU units each, tanh Gaussian policy. Adam
3e-4, batch 256. Deterministic mean actions at evaluation.

- BC: 2,000 supervised MSE updates on normalized clean expert controls.
- DAgger: same BC initialization, then 2,000-transition collection rounds with
  500 supervised updates on all accumulated data. Expert execution probability
  `0.5 * 0.5**round`; expert labels are queried at **every** collected state,
  regardless of whose action executes. Actor and optimizer persist across rounds.
- SAC backbone: 2 critics, 1,000 random warmup actions, 1 critic update per new
  transition thereafter. Target Polyak coefficient 0.005, learned entropy
  temperature initialized at 0.1, target entropy -1.5. Mean-Q actor objective
  follows the RLPD reference for **all** RL variants, so this is a documented
  SAC-style backbone rather than an untouched library SAC implementation.
- SAC + symmetric sampling: same backbone and update budget, exactly 50% prior
  and 50% online samples in each batch. Separate immutable prior buffer.
- RLPD: symmetric sampling, 10 LayerNorm critics, 4 critic updates per transition,
  1 actor/temperature update per transition. Random minimum of two target critics,
  mean ensemble actor objective. Hidden size 128 and UTD 4 are compute-conscious
  adaptations, **not an exact reproduction** of the paper's benchmark settings.
  Comparison to symmetric sampling alone changes normalization, ensemble size,
  and update budget together; it does not identify their individual effects.

Collection occurs in blocks of 32 transitions for RL. Replay capacity equals
the online interaction budget. No offline pretraining or
BC warm start is given to the reward-based methods. Initial demonstration
resources and additional DAgger expert queries are reported separately.

**Evaluation.** Every 10,000 online transitions, deterministic policy on seeds
20000–20002, with and without an intervention. At the fixed final checkpoint,
use 20000–20009 and additionally evaluate on held-out Snow Tux Peak. An
intervention overrides steering to seeded +/-0.8 for 5 actions after first
crossing a seeded progress threshold in [250,450] m. Threshold/sign are shared
across policies. This matches a track region, not speed or full simulator state.
Policies that never reach the threshold are still failures; `overridden_steps`
records whether the challenge occurred. Evaluation never adds replay or labels;
training RNG is restored afterward and a fresh training episode is started.
There is no test-driven checkpoint selection: report the fixed-budget policy.

**Analysis.** Completion rate and lap time conditional on completion, progress
for failures, learning curves versus online steps, expert queries, updates, and
wall time. Report completion beside speed to avoid survivor bias. Bootstrap
means across **independent training seeds**, never across repeated deterministic
solo laps as though they were independent training runs. Current summary uses
final available checkpoints and exposes unequal budgets. For final reporting,
restrict to completed runs at matched budgets. Five seeds give limited precision;
three-seed exploratory results warrant restrained claims. Both data variants
are shared across seeds, so uncertainty is conditional on these fixed datasets.
